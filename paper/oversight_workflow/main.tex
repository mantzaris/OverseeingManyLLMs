\title{Overseeing Many LLMs: A User-Controlled Review Queue for Concurrent Agent Workflows}
\author{\authorname{Anonymous Submission}}
\keywords{Human-Agent Interaction, Language Model Agents, Review Workflows, Concurrency, Decision Support.}
\abstract{Concurrent language model workers produce outputs that a user must inspect, defer or release while other work continues. We argue that these outputs should be individually reviewable, versioned requests with stable evidence and visible unfinished work. Our implemented desk connects asynchronous workers to a common review-and-release protocol, with a central queue and optional source sessions. A completed software evaluation uses 24 TAT-QA financial-report contexts, 145 original questions and 290 actual model answers. All 13,236 scenario events replay consistently, and active-review checks preserve the inspected output during arrivals. Ideal scripted corrections yield a mechanical completion tie, not a human benefit. Initial exact match is 61/290. A subsequent audit restores seven parser-lost displays, reducing empty or unsupported answer fields from 38 to 31 without changing exact match. The runnable formative pilot has two nine-minute queue/session blocks and a separate four-minute source-only diagnostic. No participant observations exist. This position paper contributes a specified oversight workflow, its real-data software characterization and an executable evaluation protocol. Whether source sessions improve correct review or experienced control remains to be measured.}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
A person supervising several language model workers needs to know which outputs
require a decision, what evidence supports them, and what remains unfinished.
An arriving answer should not replace the source they are reading. A postponed
question should retain its draft and history. If an agent revises an answer after
approval, the new artifact should not silently inherit permission to be released.
These are concrete requirements for a workflow in which the user retains authority over release.\credit

We investigate how a person can organize, inspect and resolve competing actionable
requests while retaining visibility of unfinished work and control of release.
Our application is financial question answering. Several independently prompted
task workers use one shared model to answer original questions about real
financial-report tables. The user can inspect the source, approve one output,
supply a correction, reject it or defer it. A shared source supports navigation,
but never implies that two questions share a correct answer.

The design favors a strong simple queue over elaborate allocation or automatic
correction propagation. Earlier internal studies found that more complicated
ordering and uncertain correction reuse did not consistently improve outcomes.
Those findings motivate separate decisions, direct evidence and explicit user
control. They do not validate this interface.

Our position is that outputs awaiting action should be explicit review objects,
rather than only entries in an agent transcript. We operationalize that position
in a review-and-release protocol, a desk with live and saved-output adapters, and
a frozen real-data software evaluation. The
protocol makes accounting, focus stability and version authority testable under
concurrent arrivals. A completed preparation package makes a formative comparison of the central queue
and optional source sessions runnable, with fundamental approval semantics common.
We present this as a position supported by an implemented system and measured
software behavior. Human review quality and experienced workload remain open
empirical questions.

\section{RELATED WORK}
Agent dashboards and intervention interfaces already have close precedents.
AgentGUI supports concurrent desks, activity views, intervention and saved
sessions. Its eight-participant evaluation concerns trace comprehension, while
a separate experiment tests automated steering \cite{agentgui}. That evidence
is stronger than ours on human interaction. Our question concerns which individual
output may be released after inspection; we have not compared the two systems.\credit

AGDebugger provides queues, pause/step controls, message edits and checkpointed
conversation forks. Its studies examine error identification and steering of
multi-agent teams \cite{agdebugger}. These mechanisms establish that queues,
history and intervention are not new contributions by themselves. Our narrower
focus is the lifecycle of individual outputs awaiting a user's release decision.

Human multi-robot supervision also has a substantial history of attention
allocation. Chien et al. compare open-queue and shortest-job-first presentation
\cite{chien}. Research on interruption recovery motivates retaining a postponed
task's context \cite{iqbal}. We apply these established principles without
interpreting queue length, token counts or scripted action time as mental demand.
These predecessors do not establish that our workflow bundle is better. Nor does
our review establish that they lack every analogous safeguard. The narrower
research position is to make release authority, active evidence and unfinished
work explicit, then evaluate them with the same source tasks and decision rules.
B is a competent ordinary queue, not a deliberately fragile baseline. A/B/C are
configurations of our application, not reproductions of the cited systems.

\section{REVIEW PROTOCOL}
\subsection{Requests, Sources and Versions}
An offered task records a worker identifier, original question, source identity
and hash, provenance, and any explicit dependencies. An actionable request is
created when its proposed answer arrives. It records the artifact version,
receipt time, requested decision and review status. Generated explanations and
citations are labeled as generated. The original source table and paragraphs
remain available even when an answer or its citation format is incomplete.\credit

The task lifecycle is offered, admitted, started and received. A received request
is queued, active, deferred, resolved or withdrawn. Artifacts are immutable version
records. A revision supersedes the current version without deleting earlier
artifacts, decisions or release history. The application does not infer dependencies
from similar wording. In the TAT-QA application, source identity is used only to
organize related questions.

No financial deadlines are invented. The protocol accepts a deadline or priority
only with an explicit origin, such as a user's instruction. Without such metadata,
the next-request recommendation is stable FIFO. Explicit deadlines use EDF within
priority. Once a review begins, the active artifact is pinned until the user acts.
The experimental observation cutoff is not a business processing deadline.

\subsection{Actions and Authority}
Approval authorizes one exact request/version. Release is a separate action and
requires approval of the current version. A user correction creates and approves
a new version of that answer only. Separating approval from release lets a user
record an inspection decision while withholding the artifact from the local released
set, for example while checking another question. Release here is an application
record, not an external financial transaction. The extra action may itself cost
effort; its usefulness has not been measured. Rejection blocks the answer and resolves the
review, but does not complete the underlying question correctly. Deferral retains
the source, draft, note and history, with an optional return time. Withdrawal is
explicit and does not erase prior events or undo any previously recorded release.

When a revised artifact arrives during review, the old source and answer remain
pinned. A visible warning offers an explicit refresh. An attempt to approve the
older version is refused. After refresh, the new version requires its own decision.
A group contains at most three already received questions with the same source
identifier. Grouping never combines their answer fields or approval authority.

Admission control pauses new task starts. It does not interrupt an in-flight model
request or prevent that request's result from arriving. All offered tasks remain
in the outstanding-work account, including those that have not started. This
distinction prevents an apparently smaller queue from concealing delayed work.

\subsection{Atomic Events and Limited Properties}
Commands are processed under one lock. Each includes an event ID, action and
payload. A repeated ID with identical content returns the earlier receipt. Reusing
an ID for different content is refused. Before applying a transition, the controller
checks its prerequisites. A refused transition restores the prior logical state.
Each command is recorded with a sequence, timestamps, result and state hash.

Let $O$ be offered tasks, $U$ unstarted tasks, $F$ in-flight tasks and $R$ received
requests. The implementation checks
\begin{equation}
 O=U+F+R.
\end{equation}
Received requests are similarly partitioned into queued, active, deferred,
resolved and withdrawn states. No accepted transition deletes a request. By
induction over transitions, every offered task therefore remains accounted for.
Immutable version records and copied active snapshots imply that a new arrival
cannot change the active reviewed artifact. A release check requires the current
version and its own approving decision, so a revision cannot inherit approval.
Grouping changes only membership and cannot authorize a sibling output.

These are structural properties under the implemented protocol. They do not imply
answer correctness, user comprehension or low workload. All releases must pass
through this controller. The local single-user server is not a distributed consensus
service or an authenticated public deployment. Journals support recovery and replay,
but are not claimed as an fsync-backed crash-atomic database. Recorded logical events
replay deterministically; fresh model outputs and timing measurements need not.

\section{SYSTEM AND INTERFACE CONDITIONS}
The backend has asynchronous task-worker and replay adapters using the same
commands. Three independently prompted workers share the existing GPU service.
The server processes model requests serially under its configured capacity, while
worker waiting, request delivery and user interaction proceed asynchronously.
An agent here is a task worker, not a separate model architecture.\credit

The browser polls public state and updates pending work without replacing the
active answer, source or text controls. It refuses older state sequences and
protects against delayed responses from a prior session. User actions and displayed
request/version identities are logged. A deferred review restores its note and
source. Saved sessions can be restored without launching workers. The ordinary
interface never loads reference annotations. A stable version prevents a decision
from being attached to content that changed while it was being inspected; it does
not establish that the inspection was adequate.

Condition A presents worker conversations with unread indicators, navigable
threads and source access. B presents a central conventional queue with one stable
active review. C adds optional bounded source sessions and admission controls.
Pending work remains visible in all three. All conditions share source access, exact-version
approval, explicit release and deferral semantics. Release guards are identical across conditions.

The prospective primary contrast is B versus C. A is a contextual baseline.
C is a workflow bundle rather than an isolated batching mechanism. The software
comparison measures its behavior under declared scripts; it does not estimate
how people choose groups, defer work or use the pause control.

\section{REAL-DATA EVALUATION DESIGN}
\subsection{Sources and Generation}
TAT-QA provides real financial-report tables and text with human-written questions
and reference annotations \cite{tatqa}. Worker assignments, source bundles,
arrival schedules and reviewer scripts are constructed for this study. This is
not a field study of financial analysts.\credit

We froze the first 24 eligible unused public test-gold contexts in source order.
The audit excluded all 36 contexts used in prior development or evaluation,
matching normalized tables and long paragraphs, and detected cross-split source
duplicates. Selected contexts have four to eight questions and a source rendering
within the existing context limit. All 145 original questions are retained.
There are no reliable complete report identifiers, so source contexts cannot be
assumed to represent independent reports or companies. Public benchmark exposure
to the model is not ruled out.

Two replicas produce 290 initial answers. The short interface requests an answer,
scale and optional explanation or citations; it does not require an executable
computation schema. The model may select a bounded arithmetic expression for a
calculator to evaluate. No reference operands or answers are inserted. Four
predetermined source rechecks produce actual revision outputs. Prompts, source
selection, generation settings, failure handling and scenarios were committed
before fresh generation.

The service is pinned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in BF16 on one RTX 6000 Ada, with zero CPU
offloading. Temperature is 0.3, top-$p$ is 1, and output allowance is 230 tokens
within a 2,048-token model context. Seeds are recorded. The saved model and source provenance, request accounting and reproduction commands
remain in the evidence package. No new inference was used to prepare the formative
pilot or this handoff. None of the original answers was replaced because of its score.

\subsection{Matched Software Scenarios}
Twelve constructed bundles each contain two source contexts. Both replicas run in
A, B and C at lower and higher arrival concentration, giving 144 scenarios.
Every task is offered at time zero. Starts become eligible at intervals of 0.15
or 0.015 seconds, followed by a 0.04-second delivery delay. At most three replay
jobs are in flight. These are compressed wall-clock test schedules, separate from
observed GPU latency. They are not assumptions about human reading speed.

A script selects one request, waits 0.095 seconds and performs a question-specific
ideal inspection. It approves a correct answer or creates an annotation-based
correction, then explicitly releases that version. The first request is deferred
once for 0.35 seconds. C pauses new starts from 0.25 to 0.65 seconds and may retain
up to three source-related questions. Actual saved rechecks enter the declared
replica-zero scenarios at 0.8 seconds. Every scenario ends at 2.2 seconds.

Methods share saved answers, sources and eligible-start schedules. C's pause can
change arrival times, but never the offered denominator. All attempts and failures
are retained. Bundle-level paired summaries average generation replicas across
12 bundles; they characterize the scripts, not people. A separate live demonstration
holds one review while real answers arrive and tests a version change without
showing annotations.

\section{RESULTS}
\subsection{Answer Quality and Scripted Completion}
The initial model achieves \ExactCount/\AnswerCount\ exact matches (21.0\%), a
mean token F1 of 22.61\%, and \JointCount/\AnswerCount\ joint answer/scale matches.
The historical projection has \EmptyCount\ empty or unsupported answer fields.
All returned bodies are parseable JSON.
Optional citations supplied in a string instead of a list trigger 157 legacy
formatting flags without removing the readable answer or source. This distinction
matters when interpreting representation validity.\credit

All conditions release all 290 answers per load after ideal scripted correction
(Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}). The script's capacity is ample at the final cutoff.
B and C therefore tie on completion in every paired bundle, with a degenerate
script interval of zero. This is a mechanical result, not human equivalence.
The low initial model accuracy makes the distinction between generated work and
ideal correction particularly important.

C does not improve scripted throughput. Its lower-load pause increases queue size
and arrival-to-selection time. Figure~\ref{fig:accounting} retains tasks that have
not started, so pausing cannot improve a completion denominator by hiding work.

\subsection{Concurrency, Versions and Latency}
All \EventCount\ scenario events replay to their recorded logical states. The
144 cutoff snapshots and released-answer scores are reconstructed independently.
There are no missing, duplicated or misassociated received request IDs in the
completed matrix and no unexpected command failures. All \StableCount\ repeated
active-snapshot checks preserve the reviewed output. These are software checks,
not independent sources or participant observations.

The live demonstration uses twelve of the initial answers from two source contexts.
A six-second scripted active review overlaps observed arrivals while new task
starts are paused for two seconds. In-flight generation continues. A fresh model
revision creates a new version, and the earlier approval attempt is refused.
The demonstration retains all twelve unresolved requests rather than claiming a
person completed them. The saved live timeline is available in the evidence package.

Across local measurements, median atomic handler time is \HandlerMedian{} ms and its 95th
percentile is \HandlerPHigh{} ms. Median browser HTTP round trip is \HttpMedian{} ms, with a 95th
percentile of \HttpPHigh{} ms. State receipt to JavaScript DOM update takes a median
\DomMedian{} ms and a 95th percentile of \DomPHigh{} ms. The latter excludes compositor paint.
Delivery lateness relative to replay timers has a 95th percentile of \DeliveryPHigh{} ms.
These boundaries exclude human response and GPU generation time.

Chromium tests pass \BrowserChecks\ checks across all interfaces, including typed
notes and focus during arrivals, source grouping, deferral, resumption, stale-state
rejection, explicit release, connection recovery and a timed study cutoff.
Figure~\ref{fig:desk} shows the source-session component.
Earlier harness and implementation defects were repaired with their failed runs
retained. The evidence package records those repairs, exact timing boundaries and
focused tests. No historical answer or result table was overwritten.





\subsection{Subsequent Answer and Display Audit}
Pilot preparation audited all 38 historical empty fields against saved raw responses.
Seven nested-array answers had been lost by the display parser. A uniform pilot-only
projection restores their existing components, leaving 31 empty or unsupported
fields. Exact match remains 61/290 and joint answer/scale correctness remains
59/290. The original 38-empty table and raw answers are preserved. Display recovery
is not an accuracy improvement.

Of 24 explicitly empty answer arrays, eight have answer-bearing text elsewhere
and four contain partial or qualified text. The pilot exposes the original response
in an expandable view without promoting explanations into scored answers. The
157 optional evidence-format flags include 32 jointly correct answers. A format
flag is therefore not a correctness label.

A reproducibly stratified audit of 22 nonempty answers includes ten correct controls
and twelve scored errors across answer types and source contexts. Nine errors show
clear content or unit problems. Three raise unresolved wording, scale or rounding
questions. These developer assessments are neither independent adjudication nor
an estimate of error causes across all 229 nonmatches. The publicly selected pilot
packets retain 36 questions from six contexts, with seven correct replica-zero
drafts and five empty fields. Selection uses public size and question features,
not model scores. Review may require reconstructing many answers. The source-only
diagnostic is necessary because the usefulness of these drafts is still unknown.

\section{RUNNABLE FORMATIVE PILOT}
\subsection{Design and Treatment}
The current prepared protocol is \texttt{formative-v1}, not a completed human study.
It has separate-source training, two nine-minute B/C blocks, and a four-minute
source-only diagnostic. The earlier interface-by-load effectiveness proposal
remains a later design option, not part of this pilot. There is one constructed
arrival schedule in B and C. The software wrapper, packets, forms, export and
analysis have been exercised with explicitly separated software fixtures. There
are no participant observations.\credit

Three packets each contain two sources and twelve original questions. Public
source length, table size and calculation wording guide selection and matching.
The six contexts are reused benchmark material, not fresh independent financial
reports. Training uses another source. Each participant sees disjoint sources in
B, C and the diagnostic. Eighteen assignments cross all six B/C/source-only orders
with three packet rotations. Coarse matching and counterbalancing do not guarantee
equal difficulty or eliminate general learning.

B and C share saved drafts, complete source access, a calculator, stable active
review, notes, individual decisions and exact-version release. C additionally
provides up to three source-session cards, session-aware next selection and
pause/resume of new task starts. Pending work remains visible in both. This is a
workflow-bundle comparison, not isolation of a grouping button. All twelve questions
are offered at block start, with starts eligible in two six-question waves at
0--20 and 180--200 seconds. Pausing does not remove work from the denominator.

The source-only block offers six original questions from its disjoint packet without
model proposals. Its position rotates. It diagnoses source comprehension, calculation
difficulty and possible reconstruction burden. Different question and time
allocations mean it is not a controlled assistance-effect estimate. A better
source-only performance cannot by itself establish that the drafts harm review,
nor can a worse performance establish that they help.

\subsection{Outcomes and Collection Boundary}
The primary descriptions are correct current-version releases, incorrect releases
and unfinished work among all offered questions. A correct release requires
official answer exact match and scale agreement. Approval without release is
separate; rejection is blocked work, not correct completion. Official exact match
and token F1 are retained, with scale agreement also reported. A blinded adjudication
workflow can add reasoned judgments on released answers, including exact-match
controls, without overwriting official scores. No such independent judgments exist.

Interaction records retain deferral, resumption, source revisits, grouping, admission
controls and focus interruptions. Select-to-action elapsed time is not continuous
reading time. Raw NASA-TLX items measure reported workload \cite{tlx}; custom control
items remain separate. No ratings are filled by scripts. The participant is the
paired B/C unit. Individual trajectories, missing blocks and packet/order imbalance
precede any interval. A small formative sample cannot establish superiority,
equivalence or population generalization across reports.

The first authorized collection should identify source or unit misunderstandings,
incorrect releases, missing-draft burden and failed resumption. A later effectiveness
study needs a meaningful target difference and variance estimates that account for
participants and sources. The prepared materials supply neither institutional
authorization nor consent. Investigator practice is development evidence, and
software fixtures cannot silently enter participant analysis. Any change after
practice or formative observations must receive a new development version before
subsequent comparison.

\section{DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION}
The workflow makes concurrent outputs addressable objects with explicit review
and release authority. Its contribution is operational: a user can keep an answer
in focus, inspect its original evidence, postpone it without losing context and
see what remains before choosing the next task. The implementation and real-data
traces make these behaviors inspectable and reproducible.\credit

Real source data matter because the desk must display authentic tables, units,
prose and imperfect answers, rather than idealized cards. They reveal missing
outputs, substantial calculation errors and annotation ambiguities that a clean
fixture would conceal. They do not supply real agent arrival patterns or measured
financial-review behavior. The ideal script reveals whether the protocol accepts
and records a correction, not whether a person can identify or produce it.

Ordinary queues and version checks remain strong explanations of the system's
reliability. No session advantage has been demonstrated. Source sessions may save
revisits, or distract from unrelated questions; separate release may support
intentional withholding, or add needless actions. Low-quality drafts may make
answering directly from the source preferable. The prepared pilot can identify
these feasibility issues, but its diagnostic block cannot quantify an assistance
effect under matched allocations.

The position is therefore specific and testable: treat concurrent agent outputs
as individual, versioned review requests, keep their evidence stable and account
for all unfinished work. We provide the implementation and measured software
behavior needed to test that position. The next milestone is real interaction
evidence from the prepared, authorized formative comparison, before a claim of
improved review quality, efficiency, perceived control or workload is made.
