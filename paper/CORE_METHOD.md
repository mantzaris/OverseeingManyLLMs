# Core method: allocating simulated supervision

## Research question

How should one simulated human supervisor allocate a limited amount of review time across three or six LLM agents when incorrect actions incur continuing cost and the opportunity to correct them expires? The implemented comparison asks whether planning review order using completion times changes loss relative to FCFS, uncertainty-first, myopic benefit, delay-aware greedy, and earliest-deadline-first. The backbone and small development batches establish integration and diagnostics; they do not establish policy superiority or novelty.

## Implemented mechanism

In the original and Stage 3 competition conditions, each agent receives two independent synthetic campus ventilation maintenance jobs during a 12-tick episode. Stage 4 adds a separate three-job, 24-tick workload. A hidden `filter` or `sensor` fault determines the correct repair. Agents see their current public job, a shared manual, two noisy clues with declared accuracies 0.8 and 0.6, costs, deadline, and their own action/review history. Hidden faults and unreleased jobs are excluded from these observations.

The pinned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model makes two independently seeded GPU generation requests for the same observation. The first action executes immediately; the second is retained only to measure agreement. Every proposal joins the review queue. Valid wrong actions remain valid model outputs. Stage 1 and development calibration collection use the **provisional, uncalibrated constant `p=0.5`**. Development validation uses a frozen empirical agreement-based estimate; no online updating occurs.

## Frozen agreement estimator

Calibration collects 16 FCFS episodes, seeds 200–215, at review duration two. Every successfully collected action pair receives an offline label: whether the **first initial proposal** was wrong, before any supervisor correction. Reviewed status does not select examples. A partial episode contributes its successfully collected pairs; missing pairs remain missing and are counted. Truth is accessed only by this offline extraction, scoring, and completed supervision.

For agreement bin `b`, estimate `p_b=(errors_b+1)/(count_b+2)`. A bin with fewer than ten examples uses the pooled calibration estimate `(total_errors+1)/(total_examples+2)`. Counts, errors, fallback decisions, source-trace hashes, and the fitted estimator hash are saved. A frozen immutable predictor accepts only the two sampled action strings, with no access to hidden state or review status.

The estimator is frozen before the first validation episode. Validation uses seeds 216–223, five policies, and review duration two: 40 episodes. Policy order rotates by scenario; sampling seeds exclude policy and execution order. Each policy uses fresh generation calls and separate histories. The full development stage has 56 episodes and 672 scheduled experimental calls, plus at most one placement generation.

In the completed batch, agreeing calibration pairs had 12 initial errors among 87 examples; disagreeing pairs had 5 among 9 and therefore triggered pooled fallback. Frozen probabilities were 13/89 for agreement and 18/98 for disagreement. The [fitted estimator](../artifacts/stage2_development/run/estimator.json) preserves these counts, provenance, and hash.

Prediction diagnostics compare the frozen estimator, constant 0.5, and the pooled calibration estimate using Brier scores on the **same saved validation trajectories**, separately by policy and agreement bin. Alternative prediction scores do not estimate counterfactual scheduling performance. Validation labels never update the estimator.

Schedulers receive only immutable public review records for released, pending jobs: proposal, estimated error probability, request time, costs, deadline, and review duration. They cannot inspect hidden faults, future jobs, scorer loss, or another agent's feedback. The supervisor performs one nonpreemptive review at a time. Reviews take two ticks in Stages 1–2; Stage 3 compares one and two ticks. Only when the review completes does it obtain definitive diagnostic information and command the correct action. The controller obeys immediately for an open job. Review cannot erase accumulated loss or reopen a closed job.

At each tick the simulator applies completed reviews, closes due jobs, releases new jobs and resolves their model calls, dispatches a review, then charges interval costs. Completion exactly at the deadline can prevent terminal loss. Inference wall time pauses the simulated clock, so hardware latency does not determine queue order. Full truth in saved scorer traces is offline diagnostic data, never a scheduler input.

## Loss and scheduling

For job `i`, release `r_i`, deadline `d_i`, downtime rate `c_i`, terminal cost `K_i`, action `a_i(t)`, and correct action `a_i*`, the episode loss sums

`loss_i = c_i Σ[t=r_i,...,d_i−1] 1[a_i(t) ≠ a_i*] + K_i 1[a_i(d_i) ≠ a_i*]`.

The preventable consequence of completing a review at `T` is

`G_i(T) = 1[T ≤ d_i] [c_i(d_i−T) + K_i]`.

All policies skip requests with no positive estimated benefit at earliest completion `t+s_i`.

| Policy | Dispatch rule for feasible requests |
| --- | --- |
| FCFS | Earliest request, with agent ID as tie-break. |
| Uncertainty-first | Highest `p_i`; shared FCFS tie-break. Constant probabilities can make it identical to FCFS. |
| Myopic benefit | Highest `p_i G_i(t)`, ignoring service time in the ranking. |
| Delay-aware greedy (`greedy`) | Highest `p_i G_i(t+s_i)`, with the shared request-time/agent-ID tie-break. |
| Earliest-deadline-first (`edf`, Stage 3) | Earliest absolute deadline, followed by the shared request-time/agent-ID/job-ID tie-break. |
| Queue-order search (`delay`) | Enumerate ordered subsets of the current queue and maximize `Σ_k p_i G_i(t+Σ_{j≤k}s_j)`; dispatch the first review and recompute when free. |

Queue-order search optimizes the current queue estimate, without anticipating arrivals. Plans tied in computed floating-point value prefer fewer reviews, then lexicographic request/agent order. Correct jobs at closure are reported alongside loss: a wrong job with both costs zero contributes zero loss.

## Assumptions and evidence

Supervision is **simulated**, with perfect diagnosis, fixed duration, and mandatory compliance. There are no human participants or measurements of workload, trust, or usability. Automatic review admission, two repair actions, independent synthetic jobs, and public deterministic deadlines/costs are simplifying assumptions. Stage 1 probabilities are uncalibrated; the development estimator is a small empirical fit with no reliability guarantee. Two samples can agree on an incorrect action.

The five [Stage 1 mechanics fixtures](../artifacts/stage1_gpu_live/rtx6000_ada_20260910T002404Z/mechanics/episodes.csv) stipulate actions to demonstrate arithmetic, correction, competition, expiry, and a baseline win. They are not model-quality observations. The [extended competition fixture](../artifacts/stage2_development/mechanics/episodes.csv) shows greedy choosing B first (loss 8) while queue search chooses A first (loss 0); these remain stipulated actions.

Stage 1's [Ada report](../reports/STAGE1_GPU_LIVE.md) and [episode CSV](../artifacts/stage1_gpu_live/rtx6000_ada_20260910T002404Z/run/episodes.csv) preserve its zero-loss tie with no corrections. The [development report](../reports/STAGE2_DEVELOPMENT.md) links the frozen estimator, live outcomes, raw requests, replay, Brier diagnostics, and dispatch competition. The eight validation scenarios are development diagnostics. Their five policy repetitions are not 40 independent scenarios and are not a final effectiveness study. The controlled Stage 3 duration comparison is described below. At the end of Stage 3, held-out evaluation and larger experiments remained later work; Stage 4 below addresses those experiments and provides a research draft.

Observed Stage 2 validation loss was 60 for FCFS/uncertainty-first and 62 for myopic/greedy/queue search, with 42/48 jobs correct and two corrections per policy. Queue search and greedy had identical review orders on all eight scenarios. Only three scenarios contained competing eligible requests. Frozen-estimator Brier score was 0.138521 versus 0.139178 for the pooled estimate and 0.25 for constant 0.5 on each policy's observed trajectory. These [diagnostics](../artifacts/stage2_development/run/prediction_diagnostics.csv) show little incremental predictive improvement over the pooled base rate; they do not establish agreement-based calibration or a scheduling advantage.

## Constructed competition condition (Stage 3)

Stage 2 had identical greedy/search orders, limited simultaneous eligible reviews, and slightly higher loss for search than FCFS. Stage 3 therefore deliberately changes the workload to investigate competition. This is a development diagnostic motivated by observed Stage 2 limitations, not an independent confirmation on the original distribution. The original generator, configurations, results, and estimator remain preserved.

For each of seeds 300–315, three first jobs arrive together at tick 0 and three second jobs at tick 6. Each wave independently permutes relative deadline windows `{2,4,5}` and terminal penalties `{4,8,12}` across the three agents. Separate seeded random streams generate deadline assignments, penalty assignments, and hidden faults/clues. Faults retain the equal binary prior and clues retain independent accuracies 0.8 and 0.6. Public urgency and penalty do not depend on hidden faults or clue correctness. Every declared scenario is included. Random permutation provides exchangeability across agent IDs; finite samples need not have exactly equal counts.

Downtime cost is zero, isolating terminal missed-deadline loss. Within the common positive-benefit eligibility guard, myopic and delay-aware greedy both rank `p_i K_i`; they are mathematically equivalent on a shared public state. Queue search may select a smaller immediate benefit to preserve multiple future completions. Earliest-deadline-first instead prioritizes urgency without weighting terminal penalties. Exhaustive scheduling itself is not claimed as novel. The experiment examines these rules inside an LLM action/feedback loop with imperfect error estimates and simulated supervision.

The Stage 2 estimator is loaded byte-for-byte and hash-verified: agreement probability `13/89`, disagreement probability `18/98` via the original sparse-bin fallback. No Stage 3 label is used to fit or update it. All six policies run on each scenario at review durations one and two: 192 episodes but only **16 paired scenario units**. Each episode receives fresh GPU calls and isolated agent histories. Scenario identity and per-sample seeds are independent of policy and duration; actual prompts can differ after different reviews. Policy order rotates and each duration runs first for eight scenarios.

The [Stage 3 declaration](../artifacts/stage3_competition/run/declaration.json) fixes source/configuration/scenario/estimator hashes, execution order, 2,304 experimental calls plus at most one placement generation, retries/timeouts, and analysis definitions before inference. Analyses separate durations and report paired scenario losses, review sequences, choices on identical saved public states, initial-action errors, and frozen-estimator Brier scores. Comparing choices on a saved state is not a counterfactual rollout. A changed review order is distinct from a reduction in realized loss. The queue illustration uses the first numerical seed with different greedy/search sequences, irrespective of the winner.

Measured results and limitations are in the [Stage 3 report](../reports/STAGE3_COMPETITION.md), [policy outcomes](../artifacts/stage3_competition/run/policy_outcomes.csv), [paired comparisons](../artifacts/stage3_competition/run/paired_comparisons.csv), and [prediction diagnostics](../artifacts/stage3_competition/run/prediction_diagnostics.csv). This narrow synthetic condition, small frozen calibration sample, binary actions, perfect simulated diagnosis, mandatory compliance, and selected review durations do not establish general effectiveness or human benefit.

All 192 Stage 3 episodes completed and replayed, with 2,304 experimental GPU generations plus one placement and no retries/failures. At review duration one, total losses were FCFS 28, uncertainty-first 28, myopic/greedy 12, and EDF/search 0. At duration two, losses were 52, 56, 52, 52, 84, and 44 respectively. Search versus greedy had scenario win/tie/loss counts 3/13/0 at one tick and 3/12/1 at two; search versus EDF had 0/16/0 and 5/9/2. Different greedy/search sequences occurred in 15/16 and 14/16 scenarios, much more often than loss differences. At two ticks every policy missed 32 review opportunities; search reduced weighted loss while leaving more jobs incorrect than greedy (11 versus 8). These are observed results of the constructed condition, not a general superiority claim.

The frozen estimator underpredicted observed initial errors (about 27–28% overall), with Brier scores around 0.211–0.219 and only 3–6 disagreement examples per policy/duration. Two groups of repeated identical requests, including sampling seeds, returned different valid actions; the cause is not established and no calls were rerun. Same-state myopic/greedy decisions and their actual review orders still matched throughout. Saved traces replay exactly, but exact regenerated GPU actions are not guaranteed by the evidence. See the [reproducibility record](../artifacts/stage3_competition/identical_request_variation.json). At the end of Stage 3, a held-out replication of the unchanged condition was the next useful step; these diagnostic outputs were not used to choose favorable evaluation scenarios.

## Stage 4: frozen research comparison

Stage 4 retains the historical generators and estimator, and adds three explicit public risk rules: the original agreement estimator, its pooled calibration probability `18/98`, and an analytical reference. The reference uses the known equal fault prior and independent clue likelihoods; it predicts the probability that the first action is wrong. Following agreeing clues gives `1/7`, following the stronger conflicting clue gives `3/11`, and the complementary repairs give `6/7` and `8/11`. Costs, hidden faults, future jobs, and validation labels are not inputs to this calculation. The reference's knowledge of the synthetic observation process is an assumption, not a general deployment capability. Both model samples remain present under every risk rule.

A separate larger workload has three or six agents, three jobs each, and 24 ticks. Waves at bases 0, 8, and 16 receive independent release jitter 0/1; deadline windows are drawn from 2/4/6, downtime rates from 0/1/3, and terminal penalties from 0/4/8/12. Stable independent per-agent/job/component streams preserve matching first-three-agent exogenous jobs across team sizes, with public parameters independent of hidden faults. All jobs close before the next wave. Queue search still sees only released requests. A checked bound of six outstanding requests supports exact enumeration of at most 1,957 ordered subsets, including empty; planning time and effort are recorded.

The new execution path fixes a numerical tie defect using `math.fsum` when accumulating candidate plan value. Historical sequential-addition search and all historical outputs remain preserved. A same-state audit of 1,920 Stage 3 dispatch records found 42 changed hypothetical search choices, all at one-tick reviews; no two-tick choice changed. This is an implementation repair, not a new scheduling objective or a rerun of historical trajectories.

One development-selected extension studies cost versus incorrect closures. Search adds `λ∈{0,4,8}` to terminal consequence in both eligibility and expected benefit. Its objective is original maintenance loss plus λ times incorrect closures. Scoring continues to report the original loss and incorrect-closure count separately, and compares common objectives at fixed weights across all methods. With equal risk and a fixed number of feasible terminal-only reviews, adding λ contributes a constant to plan value; it need not alter ordering or improve realized correctness.

Development uses seeds 400–415 in fixed pilot partitions. The full evaluation freezes original seeds 10000–10063 and competition seeds 11000–11063 across six policies and two durations, with pooled/analytical greedy/search ablations on the first 32 seeds. Larger-workload seeds 12000–12031 compare FCFS, EDF, greedy, and search at three/six agents and both durations. The extension uses separate seeds 13000–13015, competition with three agents and larger workload with six agents, all three penalties and both durations. The extension's λ=0 rows remain a separate cohort from core λ=0 rows.

Primary contrasts are search minus greedy maintenance loss at two ticks, separately on original and competition distributions, with EDF prominent as a secondary comparator. Scenario-paired bootstrap intervals use 2,000 resamples with seed 20260910. Repeated policies, jobs, and repeated requests are not independent scenarios. Fresh rollouts establish estimator-specific trajectories; alternative Brier scores on saved outputs are only prediction diagnostics. Equal seeds do not guarantee identical GPU actions, and different supervision histories can legitimately change prompts. Saved-output replay is separately deterministic.

See the [Stage 4 report](../reports/STAGE4_RESEARCH.md), [research draft](RESEARCH_DRAFT.md), [claim–evidence map](CLAIM_EVIDENCE.md), and [verified primary-source related work](RELATED_WORK.md) for completed measurements and their limits. The contribution concerns an inspectable LLM supervision experiment and analysis, not novelty of exhaustive scheduling, measured human performance, or general LLM alignment.

Stage 4 also audits floating search choices against exact rational expected values using only saved public records. Development had zero expected-value loss over 4,001 audited states, with four departures from the exact canonical tie preference. Ten uniform-risk rescaling choice differences were all exactly co-optimal. These numerical tie changes are recorded separately from informative risk-ranking changes; the frozen runtime is not redesigned during evaluation.


The completed Stage 4 session contains 2,752 frozen evaluation episodes and 304 development episodes, all replayed, with 48,320 GPU generations including 128 diagnostics and no retries/failures. At two ticks, search-minus-greedy mean loss is −0.09375 [−0.5625,0.34375] on the original 64 scenarios and −1.3125 [−2.25,−0.4375] on 64 competition scenarios (95% scenario-paired bootstrap intervals). Search and EDF tie at zero in one-tick competition. In the larger six-agent workload, search's advantage over greedy is strong at one tick (−6.1875) but nearly zero at two (−0.21875). EDF closes more jobs correctly at one tick despite higher weighted loss. These are conditional integrated-policy results, not universal superiority.

Analytical risk improves same-output Brier scores but produces small or inconsistent fresh-rollout gains. The λ extension at one-tick larger workload reduces incorrect closures while increasing original cost; at two ticks it has little count benefit, and competition is a structural null across the grid. The evaluation rational audit finds zero positive head-value regret in 34,660 public states, with 58 exact canonical-tie differences. Six eligible requests are observed live, with all 1,957 subsets examined and mean search time 4.531 ms. Thirty-eight repeated full-request groups across the session show action variation; every saved trace still replays deterministically. The [report](../reports/STAGE4_RESEARCH.md) records per-agent outcomes, positive and negative examples, complete accounting and reproduction commands.


## Adapted retail transaction review

The separate Stage 5 adapter uses pinned τ-bench retail tools, policies, task records and final-state validator semantics. Three families cover cancellation, pending-order modification and delivered-order returns/exchanges. Each agent authenticates, retrieves order/account information, inspects variants when necessary, requests exact-argument confirmation and stages one genuine mutation. Staging invokes upstream validation on a clone and does not change committed state. Target-blind account, retrieval and confirmation guards augment upstream tool checks. The scripted customer repeats its original operational information and confirms displayed proposals without independently checking backend IDs.

After preparation, three transactions enter a constructed processing batch at independently assigned public slots 0/0/1, relative cutoffs 2/4/5 and wrong-transaction weights 4/8/12. These are abstract operational processing assumptions, not measured retailer SLAs. A completed perfect simulated review at or before cutoff can replace the proposal with its private annotated target and commit it. Otherwise the proposal commits at cutoff; no later review erases that consequence. Unstaged failures have no review request and cannot be repaired by this mechanism. Reviewer approval is assumed sufficient to restore the recorded customer intent without another customer conversation; upstream tool checks run again at commitment, while exact-argument confirmation is a preparation guard.

The application loss is four points per unresolved service request plus the public processing weight if a wrong transaction commits. The expected preventable loss is therefore `p(error) * (4 + weight)` for timely completion, zero otherwise. No maintenance downtime term is used. The six application policies are no review, FCFS, EDF, uncertainty-first, delay-aware greedy and current-queue search. Search receives only released typed public requests, enumerates at most 16 ordered subsets for three requests, uses `math.fsum`, and breaks ties by fewer reviews then arrival/agent/request order. Historical policy lists and commands remain unchanged.

A separate frozen application estimator is fitted only on development staged proposals before correction, regardless of reviewed status. Public features are workflow family and non-high confidence or any earlier automatic rejection. Laplace smoothing uses `(errors+1)/(examples+2)`; bins below ten examples fall back to pooled development probability. All 64 staged calibration proposals were flagged, so the fitted rule effectively separates families rather than demonstrating confidence discrimination. It does not import maintenance probabilities or the analytical fault model.

Evaluation holds out 96 source cases/customer accounts from the 24 development cases. Broad workflow templates remain shared. Thirty-two bundles each have three cases and three fresh GPU preparation replicates. All policies and both review durations receive identical saved proposals; no model calls follow staging. These 1,152 paired policy replays isolate allocation under transaction-boundary semantics, while 288 genuine GPU workflows supply preparation variation. They are not fresh integrated retail policy rollouts. Replicates are averaged within bundle before 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples (seed 20260915); the primary contrast is search minus greedy loss at two ticks. A cheap parallel-perfect-review reference cannot fix unstaged failures.

See the [Stage 5 report](../reports/STAGE5_PRACTICAL.md), [adaptation table](RETAIL_ADAPTATION.md), [frozen declaration](../artifacts/stage5_practical/batches/evaluation/declaration.json) and [application estimator](../artifacts/stage5_practical/estimator.json). Results are an adapted benchmark study, not an official τ-bench score, a claim of unseen-template generalization or measured human supervision. The full policy/intent-error scope is reported separately from task completion and from automatically blocked calls.


The frozen retail evaluation retains all 288 workflow attempts: 233 staged proposals, 51 initial errors and 55 unstaged failures. All 1,152 paired policy traces replay. At two ticks, search-minus-greedy mean loss is +0.2500 [−0.3333, 1.0000] over 32 bundle means (one search win, 29 ties, two losses). Search performs 190 reviews versus greedy's 169, but completes 222 tasks versus 223. At one tick every review policy corrects all queued initial errors, leaving mean loss 2.2917 entirely from unstaged failures. Thus the practical study does not establish a search advantage. Aggregate Brier score improves over pooled calibration risk, but the modification/return-family ranking is reversed on evaluation. These are conditional transaction-boundary findings with fixed perfect simulated review, not evidence about real human supervision.


## Retail corrective-authority and review-time robustness

Stage 6 adds isolated paths in `overseeing/retail/robustness.py`; historical simulation and estimators are unchanged. Full correction can prevent `4 + W` loss when timely. A reviewer with perfect detection but approve-or-block authority can prevent only `W`: it approves a correct proposal unchanged or blocks a wrong one, leaving service unresolved and committed state unchanged. Expected scheduling benefit is therefore `p * W`, with no completion credit for blocking. Hidden labels are consulted only at completed review and offline scoring. Equality at cutoff remains timely.

A separate one-factor condition uses `base duration + 1` when the public proposed `item_ids` list has at least two entries. It keeps correction authority. No authority-by-complexity interaction is evaluated. This simple burden proxy is constructed, not a measured human service time; omissions can make a wrong proposal look simpler. Staging, full-transaction confirmation, family-specific processing stages, consequence weights, exact state equality and preparation scope are examined in the [assumption review](RETAIL_ASSUMPTION_REVIEW.md).

The all-split account audit selects 24 fresh cases in eight bundles, with three preparation replicates, after excluding every Stage 5 account. The same preparations supply six policies, both base capacities and all three conditions. The primary search-minus-greedy contrast under approve/block at base two is +1.6667 [0.0000, 4.6667], with zero wins, six ties and two losses. All 675 GPU attempts succeed; 21 of 72 workflows remain unstaged. All 864 policy traces replay. Reference and restricted authority have identical fresh sequences, so their difference in task completion and part of their loss difference follows from authority rather than improved ordering.

The separate post hoc analysis uses all 288 earlier preparations and 3,456 traces. Its restricted-authority difference is +0.1250 [-0.1667, 0.5000]. It does not replace Stage 5's original +0.2500 [-0.3333, 1.0000] primary result. Neither fresh nor post hoc results justify general search superiority. Probability scoring is conditional on staging; no follow-up label changes the frozen estimator. See the [report](../reports/STAGE6_ROBUSTNESS.md), [frozen declaration](../artifacts/stage6_robustness/batches/evaluation/declaration.json), and [reproduction guide](STAGE6_REPRODUCTION.md).


## Measured HVAC and fallible review

The principal externally grounded application uses actual FLEXLAB SZCAV/SZVAV sensor windows and experimentally imposed day labels. It is separate from the invented ventilation clues and simulated retail records. Whole-day splitting yields eight development and 18 evaluation blocks on one air handler. Three windows per block remain correlated. Source labels, dates and filenames never enter proposal, review or scheduling inputs.

Review may correct, preserve, harm or abstain. Its expected timely benefit is weight times the probability of a correction minus the probability of introducing an unresolved outcome. The development-only signed estimate can be nonpositive, in which case all policies share a decision to decline review. A risk-only ablation ignores harm, and ideal correction bounds effectiveness. All conditions share saved proposal/review pairs; answers take effect only at review completion.

Actual diagnostics are 19/54 initially correct, 14/54 after every model review, and 27/54 for a development-fitted conventional baseline. The primary signed-gain condition declines all review. Ideal search beats greedy but matches EDF under the simple cutoff structure. These observations support assessing review effectiveness before attributing value to queue planning. Simulated weights and cutoffs do not establish equipment damage, repair success or operational savings. See [Stage 7 assumptions](HVAC_ASSUMPTIONS.md), [report](../reports/STAGE7_EMPIRICAL.md), and [reproduction](STAGE7_REPRODUCTION.md).
