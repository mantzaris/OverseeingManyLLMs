\title{Planning Shared Review for Language Model Workflows with Expiring Intervention Opportunities}
\author{\authorname{Anonymous Submission}}
\keywords{Language Model Agents, Review Scheduling, Decision Support, Tool Workflows, Resource Allocation.}
\abstract{A shared reviewer must decide which language model proposals can benefit from intervention before their review opportunities expire. We formulate allocation with finite service time and application-specific expected preventable loss, including corrections and harmful reviews. Our principal case study uses measured Berkeley Lab air-handler observations with experimentally imposed fault labels. A frozen evaluation covers 18 whole-day blocks on one laboratory unit. The model diagnoses 19 of 54 windows correctly, versus 27 for a conventional classifier. Model review corrects one error but makes six correct proposals unresolved. A development-fitted signed-benefit rule declines review, producing a mechanical policy tie. With ideal review, search reduces modeled loss relative to greedy by 3.333 points, but earliest-deadline-first matches search. Controlled synthetic maintenance provides complementary evidence of conditional planning benefits and objective tradeoffs. Adapted retail workflows retain unfavorable search-minus-greedy results of +0.250 and +1.667 under two intervention authorities. Together, the studies show how capacity, risk ranking and corrective effectiveness determine whether review ordering matters, while separating externally validated task correctness from assumed oversight dynamics.}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\noindent Several agents may each have a reasonable request for assistance, yet one reviewer cannot address them all before their actions take effect. Reviewing the request with the largest immediate expected benefit may consume the only remaining slot for another useful intervention. Conversely, planning an elaborate review order may accomplish no more than earliest-deadline-first when capacity is sufficient, or may protect a correct transaction while a later mistake expires.\draftcredit

These possibilities arise in workflows where intervention has a service time and a boundary beyond which it cannot undo a consequence. A maintenance correction can stop future downtime without erasing downtime already incurred. A retail approval gate can prevent a mistaken transaction from being posted, but may lack authority to replace it with the intended transaction. The value of a review therefore depends on when it finishes and what the reviewer is allowed to do.

We ask when planning the order of a finite shared review queue improves outcomes over greedy benefit allocation and earliest-deadline-first. We also examine how risk estimates, capacity and the performance objective qualify any benefit. Our principal application uses measured laboratory HVAC observations and saved model diagnoses and reviews. A synthetic maintenance environment isolates scheduling mechanisms, while executable retail workflows test transfer and intervention limits.

The contributions are threefold. First, we give an executable formulation that connects public review requests to an application-specific preventable-loss function, while keeping hidden correctness labels outside scheduling. Second, we implement complementary protocols for feedback-dependent maintenance trajectories and allocation on identical saved HVAC judgments or retail transactions. Third, we provide externally grounded diagnosis evidence alongside frozen paired planning gains, null results and unfavorable transfer results. The measured study makes reviewer effectiveness a tested component rather than an exclusively perfect assumption. Exact enumeration supplies an interpretable small-queue planner. The contribution concerns the formulation, integration and empirical analysis rather than a new exhaustive scheduling algorithm.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\noindent Help-seeking, communication and constrained deferral address related parts of the oversight problem. KnowNo uses calibrated prediction sets to decide when a language model planner should request help \cite{ren2023knowno}. Its request-admission mechanism and coverage assumptions differ from ordering an already admitted queue. Our uncertainty-first baseline ranks estimated error probability; it does not reproduce KnowNo or provide a conformal guarantee.\draftcredit

Value of Information evaluates clarification by its expected decision utility relative to communication cost \cite{dong2026voi}. We similarly express intervention value through an explicit objective, but allocate a reviewer among competing pending transactions rather than choose a clarification question. One Human, N Agents studies a limited audit budget under miscalibrated and correlated confidence \cite{zavattari2026onehuman}. That work directly establishes shared auditing as prior research. Our focus is finite-duration intervention before a posting or correction cutoff, including the difference between blocking an error and completing a task.

DeCCaF learns expert correctness and uses cost-sensitive constrained allocation across experts \cite{alves2024deccaf}. We retain an ideal-review reference and separately measure corrections and harm from a model reviewer on sensor evidence. These are conceptual comparisons of mechanisms, not reproductions of those complete systems. Author-linked implementations of KnowNo, Value of Information and DeCCaF were inspected. No author-linked released implementation for One Human, N Agents was located in the checked paper and project searches.

Classical scheduling already studies deadlines, processing time and weighted consequences. Hariri et al. consider weighted late work, meaning processing performed after a due date \cite{hariri1995latework}. Guo et al. analyze a Pareto tradeoff between weighted tardy-job counts and weighted late work \cite{guo2022pareto}. Our losses differ, but the connection explains why urgency, count objectives and cost objectives can favor different schedules. Exhaustive current-queue search is an established method for a bounded scheduling problem.

The retail substrate is $\tau$-bench, which supplies interactive tasks, domain policies, database tools and annotated final-state validation \cite{yao2024tau}. We reuse its pinned retail implementation while changing the user interaction and adding staging, cutoffs and review. This adaptation makes transaction review testable while retaining genuine retrieval, item selection, payment and mutation semantics.

\section{REVIEW ALLOCATION MODEL}
\noindent A released review request $i$ exposes an arrival time $a_i$, a cutoff $d_i$, a proposed action, review duration $s_i$, public consequence parameters and an estimated initial-error probability $p_i$. The scheduler sees only released requests. It cannot inspect a hidden correct action, a future job or a future model output. Each nonpreemptive reviewer processes one request at a time. The measured HVAC application also varies the number of reviewer slots. A review result becomes available only when its service finishes.\draftcredit

Let $G_i(T)$ be the preventable consequence if request $i$ is initially wrong and an effective review completes at time $T$. The expected benefit is
\begin{equation}
 B_i(T)=p_iG_i(T).
 \label{eq:benefit}
\end{equation}
Eligibility requires release and positive benefit from starting now, $B_i(t+s_i)>0$. A cutoff equality is timely. Consequences incurred before completion remain incurred. This expression assumes effective review. The HVAC study replaces error risk with signed expected improvement to include ineffective and harmful model reviews. Ideal correction remains a reference, and retail separately varies corrective authority.

\subsection{Policies and Planning}
FCFS selects the earliest eligible arrival. Earliest-deadline-first (EDF) selects the earliest cutoff. Uncertainty-first selects the largest $p_i$. Delay-aware greedy selects the largest $B_i(t+s_i)$. Queue-order search enumerates ordered subsets $\pi$ of the eligible current queue and maximizes
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=1}^{|\pi|} B_{\pi_k}\left(t+\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{\pi_j}\right).
 \label{eq:search}
\end{equation}
It dispatches the first request and replans when free. It does not plan with unreleased arrivals or hidden labels, and it is not a full-horizon optimal-control solution. Ties prefer fewer reviews and then the declared lexicographic arrival, agent and request order. Value sums use stable summation. Historical numerical audits distinguish exactly co-optimal tie differences from positive decision regret.

There are $\sum_{k=0}^{q}q!/(q-k)!$ ordered subsets for $q$ eligible requests. This is 16 at three requests and 1,957 at six. We enumerate them all without queue truncation. Retail additionally includes a no-review control with the same automatic safeguards. Maintenance includes a myopic-benefit baseline; its terminal-only greedy equivalence is checked rather than presented as independent evidence.

\subsection{Pairing and Interpretation}
An exogenous scenario fixes jobs or source cases, arrivals, deadlines and costs. A policy run is not an independent scenario. Maintenance conditions share exogenous streams but receive fresh generations and separate feedback histories. Retail conditions share a saved prepared transaction within a generation replicate because no model generation follows staging. HVAC conditions similarly share prepared diagnoses and review responses. These comparisons isolate allocation on identical inputs, while synthetic maintenance measures integrated trajectories that may also differ in model behavior.

For each declared primary comparison, we compute scenario-level paired loss differences and a percentile interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples. Retail first averages three generation replicates within each source bundle. All conditions for a scenario are resampled together. Other contrasts are secondary, with no multiplicity adjustment. A wide interval spanning zero is uncertainty about the effect, not evidence of equivalence.

\section{SYNTHETIC MECHANISM STUDY}
\noindent A separate civilian maintenance simulation exposes binary faults through invented noisy clues with accuracies 0.8 and 0.6. It is not measured ventilation data. Two fresh model samples accompany each job; the first executes and the second supplies agreement information. Completed ideal supervision can correct an action and change later feedback histories. An error accumulates downtime until correction or closure, plus a terminal penalty. Public arrivals, costs and deadlines are independent of hidden faults.\draftcredit

The frozen evaluation contains 2,752 episodes across original, constructed competition, larger workloads and an objective extension. The supplement preserves complete distributions, risk definitions, matrices and results. Search minus greedy at two-tick service is $-0.094$ [$-0.563,0.344$] on 64 original scenarios and $-1.313$ [$-2.250,-0.438$] on 64 competition scenarios. Competition releases three requests together and uses terminal penalties only. Search beats EDF there by 2.563 points at two ticks, while both reach zero loss at one tick.

On the six-agent workload, search minus greedy is $-6.188$ [$-9.376,-3.281$] at one tick and $-0.219$ [$-2.313,1.969$] at two ticks. Lower capacity does not monotonically increase the value of planning. Analytical risk improves prediction scores without consistent allocation gains. Adding an incorrect-closure penalty reduces wrong closures at one tick while increasing original cost. These positive, null and unfavorable findings illustrate mechanisms and objective tradeoffs without providing external task validation.

\section{RETAIL TRANSFER AND INTERVENTION LIMITS}
\noindent The retail application adapts pinned $\tau$-bench tools, policies and task records \cite{yao2024tau}. Customer and transaction records are simulated. GPU-generated agents retrieve accounts, interpret policies, confirm intent and propose validated cancellations, modifications or returns/exchanges. Automatic guards remain enabled. Completed simulated review may intervene at an added staging boundary before a constructed cutoff. This adaptation does not produce an official benchmark score.\draftcredit

The operational objective combines an unresolved-service cost of four with a dimensionless wrong-posting weight. Original ideal review can replace a staged transaction with its annotated target. A restricted reviewer can instead approve or block, preventing a wrong posting while leaving the service request unresolved. A separate complexity condition adds one tick for multi-item proposals. Customer reconfirmation after replacement, real processing cutoffs and empirical consequence weights remain unresolved assumptions in the source-based methodological review.

The original evaluation uses 96 source cases in 32 bundles, three generation replicates and 288 GPU workflows. All 1,152 policy replays share preparations within each replicate. Fifty-five unstaged failures remain outside the review boundary. At one tick every review policy corrects every staged error. At two ticks, search minus greedy mean loss is $+0.250$ with a 95\% bundle-paired interval [$-0.333,1.000$], one bundle win, 29 ties and two losses. Search completes 222 jobs versus greedy's 223 and has mean loss 3.708 versus 3.458. This primary result is unfavorable to search.

The robustness follow-up excludes all 120 previously used accounts across upstream splits. It evaluates 24 fresh cases in eight bundles, again with three replicates. Under restricted authority and two-tick service, search minus greedy is $+1.667$ [0.000,4.667], with zero wins, six ties and two losses. Search blocks eight wrong transactions versus greedy's twelve, while both complete the same 39 initially correct tasks. Twenty-one unstaged failures remain. Full correction and complexity time both give a $+2.333$ search-minus-greedy difference, although complexity raises both losses. Post hoc reuse of the original preparations is reported separately in the supplement.

These negative results have explanatory value. Preserving a public review opportunity can prioritize a correct transaction while a wrong one expires. Restricted authority changes which consequence is preventable without necessarily changing review order. More completed reviews do not imply more corrected tasks. The main claims therefore retain both unfavorable retail comparisons rather than treating synthetic planning gains as a universal result.

\section{DISCUSSION}
\noindent Planning helps when interventions are effective, compete for time and ordering preserves opportunities that greedy selection would lose. The measured application shows why effectiveness must be checked first. A frozen signed-gain estimate declines harmful model review; ideal review creates an ordering benefit that EDF already captures in the simple deadline structure. Constructed maintenance competition provides that condition. The original maintenance workload and the retail study show why a favorable mechanism does not imply a general policy ranking. Public contention can involve correct requests, some errors are already infeasible, and later arrivals are outside the planner's current state.\draftcredit

Capacity changes the opportunity set. When every useful queued error can be corrected, simple review policies can match search despite different orders. When capacity is severely constrained, too few feasible combinations may remain for ordering to help. More completed reviews can then coexist with fewer corrections. The meaningful outcome is preventable operational consequence, not sequence diversity alone.

The reviewer objective and authority must also be explicit. A search policy that optimizes weighted cost can close fewer jobs correctly. A block-only reviewer can prevent processing harm while leaving service unfinished. These outcomes are consistent with their objectives and should not be converted into claims of universal superiority. The robustness study isolates these mechanisms without changing risk estimates or agent preparation.

Our evidence is limited to one model and bounded workloads. Measured HVAC experiments cover one laboratory unit, two control modes and deliberately imposed faults. Their 18 evaluation days are correlated through common equipment, and both diagnosis methods miss the cooling class. Model review is weak and correlated with proposal errors. Synthetic faults supply a transparent mechanism study, while retail records offer executable tools but shared templates and possible training exposure. Scripted confirmation and complete-transaction correction simplify customer interaction. Deadlines, processing weights and complexity times are constructed quantities. Exact annotated equality can overstate some operational distinctions. No measured human review performance, retailer savings or large-queue scalability follows.

\section{CONCLUSION}
\noindent Shared review is a timed intervention problem with an application-specific preventable-loss function. Measured HVAC evidence establishes reviewer effectiveness as a central bottleneck. A development-fitted signed-benefit rule declines model reviews that often introduce errors, while ideal review creates an ordering benefit already captured by EDF. Synthetic maintenance supplies complementary planning gains and objective tradeoffs. Both retail primary comparisons remain unfavorable to search. Together, these results support analyzing capacity, risk ranking and corrective authority alongside scheduling. External diagnosis labels establish task correctness, while operational consequences remain assumptions. The framework makes this separation reproducible without claiming human performance or real savings.\draftcredit
