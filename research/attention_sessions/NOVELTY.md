# Novelty assessment and claim boundaries

**Current decision: follow-up interaction research; no new scheduling superiority
or reduced-human-workload claim.** The selected prototype is useful because it
exposes a testable decision contract and its failure modes. Its component
combination alone does not establish novelty.

| Proposed claim | Strongest competing explanation | Evidence and status |
|---|---|---|
| Grouping related requests saves repeated setup | Classical family scheduling and notification batching | Established prior mechanism. Equal reuse across singleton methods removes an easy but misleading advantage. Not claimed as new. |
| A deadline certificate makes grouping safer than simple ordering | Sticky EDF can preserve the same local continuation without group coordination | Primary guarded-minus-sticky loss +2 [0,6], 0/5/1. Certificate is local and does not protect the stronger baseline's entire realized sequence. No superiority claim. |
| A visible, editable commitment helps users retain control | HiLSVA provenance/session controls, mixed initiative, bounded deferral and ordinary persistent queues | Specific interaction hypothesis remains untested by people. Prototype and protocol operationalize it; source review does not establish priority over all related systems. |
| Shared evidence can answer several agents at once | MAC global clarification, VoI belief updates and HiLSVA knowledge reuse | Rejected as the main direction on these data. Three windows of one record are distinct diagnoses, not a common intent. No answer propagation in the prototype. |
| Adaptive presentation reduces demand | Exact AgentLens v3 already supplies controlled human interaction evidence | Alternative examined, not built. Cached individual responses cannot estimate the effect of changed presentation. |
| Fewer session offers reduce cognitive load | Offers, duration and queue length are simulator proxies | No human evidence. The future protocol measures behavior and self-reports separately. |
| Risk-based intervention improves outcomes | DeCCaF models expert correctness; One Human, N Agents studies ineffective audit regimes | Stage 7 saved model review is harmful overall and signed gains admit none. Preserved, not overcome by grouping. |

The literature matrix distinguishes implemented comparators from conceptual
references. The capacity-allocation baseline is a deliberately reduced model,
not a full DeCCaF reproduction. Our certificate is an explicit feasibility check
under declared timing, not a theorem of optimal control. The study contributes
a reproducible negative comparison and a concrete interaction test, with no
algorithmic priority assertion.

Historical interpretations remain unchanged: the retail studies do not show a
search advantage; Stage 7's primary model policies mechanically tie with no
reviews; its reviewer introduces more harm than correction; ideal EDF matches
ideal search in primary timing; and risk-only greedy reaches the same HVAC
outcome with fewer reviews than search. None establishes cognitive-load reduction.

**Would justify extension later:** domain-observable cases, observed evidence
reuse, reliable individual decisions, understood certificate limits, fewer lost
deferrals and an acceptable quality/effort tradeoff against a polished sticky-EDF
interface. A later preregistered study must distinguish the effect of the session
from its explanatory text if claiming the certificate itself helps.

**Would falsify or narrow it:** no context-reconstruction savings, frequent
misgrouping, poorer decisions within groups, costs exceeding saved effort,
misunderstood protection promises, or equal quality with simpler presentation.
The present pilot already falsifies a general allocation advantage over the
strongest simple comparator on its declared primary condition.
