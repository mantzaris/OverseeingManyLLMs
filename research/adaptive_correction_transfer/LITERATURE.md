## Narrow claim to test

The candidate protocol combines inspection acquisition and scoped transfer through one explicit feedback boundary. It keeps a before/after ledger for each receiving output. A later purchased inspection can identify a beneficial or harmful transition for that question; the selector may then update its model. Uninspected private scores cannot serve as rewards. Observable context/evidence relations identify candidates, not certified applicability.

The strongest competing explanation is that a competent risk-ranked individual correction or fixed source-aware memory captures the available value. Another is that extra generations alone explain improvements. The declared experiment must include both. A result where the adaptive and fixed policies make identical decisions, or where direct reference replacement accounts for every gain, does not establish a new adaptive-transfer contribution.

Applicability and conditional repair success are not separately identifiable from the available outcome labels. We model observable transition rates, including harm, and state that limit rather than assigning a calibrated probability to a hidden root cause. No correctness or human-workload guarantee follows from a posterior score.
