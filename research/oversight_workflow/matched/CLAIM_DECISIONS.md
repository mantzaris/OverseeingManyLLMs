# Outcome-to-claim rule

Primary hypothesis: G increases joint-correct current-version releases per offered
question relative to Q. It is not a current finding. Q-M and G-M test assistance
with these drafts and tasks under equal allocations.

| Observed outcome | Permitted report | Claim that remains unsupported |
|---|---|---|
| A. G improves correct completion with informative uncertainty | Effect in percentage points and absolute answers, paired uncertainty, errors, task scope and event-linked mechanism evidence | Universal grouping benefit or mental-effort reduction from log proxies |
| B. G/Q accuracy uncertain or similar but resumption/source use/control differs | The specific measured interaction or self-report difference, its uncertainty and limits | Equivalence in accuracy, or accurate review because users report control |
| C. G improves correct completion and raises incorrect releases | Both effects and the explicit quality/error tradeoff | Unqualified better oversight or safer release |
| D. M matches or exceeds assisted conditions | Draft usefulness is limited under these tasks, model outputs and allocations | General failure of LLM assistance, or equivalence from a nonsignificant test |
| E. Inconclusive comparative results | Individual outcomes, missingness, uncertainty and concrete usability/task findings | An upgraded effectiveness claim |
| F. No genuine matched participant records | Working system, matched protocol and software verification; current position paper | Completed comparative human evidence |

The present state is **F**. There are no participant figures, invented ratings or
qualitative themes. Software fixtures and investigator practice are explicitly
excluded from independent comparative evidence. A prepared design does not make a
regular empirical paper. Keep the existing manuscript intact until actual findings
support revision. A later revised draft should explain the specific interaction
mechanism relative to the existing AgentGUI/AGDebugger comparisons, not claim that
queues, grouping or version checks themselves are new.

Any superiority claim must report errors and unfinished work alongside correct
releases. No noninferiority margin or power claim is declared in this formative
version. Informative uncertainty depends on the realized data and sampling design;
it is not guaranteed by completing six assignments. Changes to tasks/interfaces
following observation require a separately identified development version, and
versions must not be pooled to obtain a favorable result.
