## What was consolidated

The eight-page draft now argues for concurrent outputs as individually reviewable,
versioned requests with stable evidence and visible unfinished work. It explains
why approval can be recorded before a separate release, and why an arrival or
revision must not silently change the object being reviewed. It also acknowledges
that these are familiar software mechanisms whose additional human value needs
testing. AgentGUI and AGDebugger are credited for existing observation, steering,
queue and history capabilities. No head-to-head advantage or first-interface
claim is made.

The completed software study remains 24 contexts, 145 questions, two replicas and
290 initial Qwen answers, with 61 exact matches. The original evaluation retains
38 empty/unsupported fields. The pilot display projection restores seven nested
arrays, leaving 31; exact match stays 61/290 and joint answer/scale correctness
59/290. Formatting flags are not correctness labels. The bounded 22-case audit
and its three unresolved disputes remain developer assessments. None was silently
turned into a correct answer.

The paper separates three evidence levels:

| Evidence | Completed status | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Asynchronous live integration and 144 saved-output scenarios | Historical results preserved | Stable review, version authority and work accounting; ideal scripted completion ties |
| Offline answer audit and executable formative-pilot preparation | Completed, code/manifest frozen | Real source/draft feasibility is inspectable; collection/export/analysis machinery works |
| Human B/C effectiveness, perceived control and workload | **No observations** | Unestablished; scripts and practice do not supply participant evidence |

The current pilot has two nine-minute B/C blocks and a four-minute, six-question
source-only diagnostic. It uses disjoint packets and counterbalanced orders. The
older interface-by-load effectiveness proposal is retained as a later option,
not pooled with the formative design. The diagnostic's different question/time
allocations cannot estimate an assistance effect. It can expose source difficulty
and the need to reconstruct weak drafts.

## Handoff decision

1. **Ready for author review:** the active eight-page position paper, claim/evidence
   table, three-objection reviewer memo, frozen pilot, short walkthrough and blank
   feedback form. Launch, export and separate analysis commands are in README.md.
2. **Scientifically unestablished:** human review quality, efficiency, perceived
   control and workload benefits, the value of C over B, and whether weak drafts
   help more than they require reconstruction.
3. **Try now:** open http://127.0.0.1:9042 locally, verify investigator-practice mode,
   select assignment 7 and follow the walkthrough. Keep any actual feedback as
   development evidence; an abbreviated practice run is not a B/C comparison.
4. **Change only for observed reasons:** reproducible loss of a note/version,
   unclear approval/release authority, overlooked offered work, inaccessible source
   information or repeated task/units misunderstandings would justify a documented
   version change. A preference for one screen alone is not effectiveness evidence.
5. **First authorized formative collection:** investigate whether people can check
   these original sources and drafts, avoid incorrect releases, resume unfinished
   work, and use the extra C controls meaningfully. Diagnose source-only difficulty
   and measurement variability before specifying an effectiveness study.
