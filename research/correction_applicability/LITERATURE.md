## Candidate claim and falsifier

The candidate is a question-specific correction-to-patch protocol that preserves an immutable source, identifies the affected computation component, tests a recipient's explicit contract, and either commits a minimal revision or rolls back. Its useful distinction would be **additional correct uninspected outputs attributable to the acquired correction**, at the same inspection boundary and beyond generic executable verification. Typed records, version hashes, dependency tracking, transactional updates and arithmetic checks are not new by themselves.

The strongest alternative explanation is better source reading or arithmetic execution. The independent generic-verification and execution-only controls receive the same source, representation interface and checks, but no sibling disclosure. Ordinary reattempts isolate extra generation. A gate failure or superiority only over broad regeneration supports a simpler verification method, not a new correction-reuse contribution. No model-weight editing, calibrated semantic guarantee, human-workload claim or multi-agent architectural advantage is asserted.
