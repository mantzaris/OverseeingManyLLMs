 Planning Shared Review for Language Model Workflows with Expiring
                     Intervention Opportunities

                                                Anonymous Submission



Keywords:        Language Model Agents, Review Scheduling, Decision Support, Tool Workflows, Resource Allocation.

Abstract:        A shared reviewer must decide which language model proposals can benefit from intervention before their
                 review opportunities expire. We formulate allocation with finite service time and application-specific ex-
                 pected preventable loss, including corrections and harmful reviews. Our principal case study uses measured
                 Berkeley Lab air-handler observations with experimentally imposed fault labels. A frozen evaluation covers
                 18 whole-day blocks on one laboratory unit. The model diagnoses 19 of 54 windows correctly, versus 27
                 for a conventional classifier. Model review corrects one error but makes six correct proposals unresolved. A
                 development-fitted signed-benefit rule declines review, producing a mechanical policy tie. With ideal review,
                 search reduces modeled loss relative to greedy by 3.333 points, but earliest-deadline-first matches search.
                 Controlled synthetic maintenance provides complementary evidence of conditional planning benefits and ob-
                 jective tradeoffs. Adapted retail workflows retain unfavorable search-minus-greedy results of +0.250 and
                 +1.667 under two intervention authorities. Together, the studies show how capacity, risk ranking and cor-
                 rective effectiveness determine whether review ordering matters, while separating externally validated task
                 correctness from assumed oversight dynamics.
